Same Page First

The first project management skill: agree on the words.

Project

Temporary. Specific goal.
A start and an end.

Thesis: yes. School fair: yes.
Running a store day to day: no, that is operations.

Nanay's sinigang for lunch? A project: specific goal, starts with the ingredient list, ends when the family eats at noon.

Management

  • Deciding what, by whom, by when, with what resources
    PlanningOrganizing
  • Adjusting when reality surprises the plan
    MonitoringControllingRisk Management
  • Not bossing people around
    LeadingDelegating

The Sinigang Project

The projectMom cooks sinigang for lunch. Temporary, specific goal, clear end.
To-do list1. Buy the ingredients, with a list and a budget. 2. Cook the sinigang.
ScopeSinigang for the whole family. Nothing more, nothing less.
CostIngredient money, plus gas or electricity for cooking
TimeAbout 2 hours, groceries plus cooking, done by 12 noon
DelegationYou buy the ingredients. Mom cooks. Mom answers for lunch.
Progress tracking"Nabili mo na ba?" at 11AM. That text is a status check.
Risk managementBuy late? Everybody eats late, at mapapalo ka ni mama.

A group reaching a temporary goal ON PURPOSE

Planned, coordinated, adjusted. Not luck.

The formal definitionThe structured practice of planning, organizing, and executing tasks to achieve a specific goal within defined limits of time, cost, and quality.

The Project Lifecycle

InitiationDefining the primary goal, scope, and feasibility of a new idea
PlanningCreating a roadmap that outlines schedules, budgets, resource allocation, and risk management
ExecutionCoordinating people and resources to complete the actual work
Monitoring and ControllingTracking progress and performance to ensure the project stays on schedule and budget
ClosingDelivering the final output, evaluating results, and formally wrapping up the project

Section 02

1,100 Forms.
5 Months.

A true story from my first day at a new company.

The Commitment

  • Convert a legacy Visual Basic 6 system to .NET
  • 1,100 forms promised in 5 months
  • 7 engineers: 6 writing code, 1 QA
  • No documentation: the old app, one visiting expert, and clicking around
  • Client delegates had already booked flights for training at month 5

Month 1: The Scoreboard

10 of 1,100 forms

And those 10 were still buggy.

At that pace, 5 months10 x 5 = 50
Fantasy mode: 1 form per engineer per day, zero rest days30 x 7 x 5 = 1,050
The promise1,100

Even the fantasy misses the promise.

The Red Flags I Found on Night One

  • RISK  Windows Forms quietly became WEB forms mid-flight. The estimates never changed.
  • RISK  A framework NOBODY on the team knew. Even the tech lead struggled with it.
  • RISK  5 months was promised while honest estimates were still being computed.
  • NO PM  The team had been requesting a project manager for months. There was none.
  • COMMUNICATION  The team kept saying 5 months was impossible. Nobody listened.

We Tried to Out-Code the Problem

  • Day two: I was made assistant team lead
  • Sorted all 1,100 forms by complexity: easiest CRUD forms first
  • Wrote best practices and coding standards for the new framework
  • A month of overnights: 110 forms, less buggy. 11x faster
  • But 110 x 3 remaining months = 330. Heroics do not beat arithmetic.

Section 03

Why Talent Is Not Enough

Engine and Steering

Talent multiplies output.
It does not choose the goal.

Talent is the engine.
Project management is the steering.

TIME SCOPE COST QUALITY

Scope. Time. Cost. Quality is carried by all three.

capacity = people x time

Possible work must meet demanded work.

If Nobody Moves a Lever

  • Corners get cut
  • Testing gets skipped
  • People burn out
  • Quality pays the bill you refused to pay

Brooks's Law

Adding people to a late project makes it later.

Onboarding plus coordination eats the gain.

Which Lever Would You Move?

  • Sponsor moves the event 2 weeks earlier
  • A thesis groupmate drops the subject
  • Client doubles the features, not the pay

Vote by hands: scope, time, or cost?

Five Hats on Every Project

Stakeholderswant the outcome, define success
PM / Product Ownerowns what and when
Tech Leadowns how
Team Membersown the work
QAowns "does it really work?"

Responsibility vs Accountability

Responsibility

I DO the work.
Can be shared.

Accountability

I ANSWER for the result.
One name only.

Five responsible members. Zero accountable ones. Until 2AM.

Delegation Is Not Dumping

Dumping

The task only.

"Make the event poster. Due tomorrow."
No files. No budget. No say. No help.

Delegating

The task, the authority, the context.

"You own the poster. Theme and logos are in this folder. Budget: 500 pesos, design is your call. Due Friday. Ping me if you get stuck."

CLARITY

Clear goal. Clear priorities. Clear owners. Clear done.

State the Obvious

  • Silent nods are how teams fail politely
  • Rule 1: nobody feels stupid for raising the obvious
  • Rule 2: never question a teammate's capacity for asking
  • Questions are cheap. Assumptions are expensive.

Many Frameworks, One Discipline

  • Scrum: short cycles, what you learned today
  • Kanban: continuous flow, limit work in progress
  • Waterfall: plan everything first, right for fixed known work
  • The discipline: shared words, shared plan, clear owners, short feedback loops

The Mini-Scrum (photograph this)

  1. Write everything the project needs: your backlog
  2. Order it, pick the MVP
  3. ONE accountable owner per item
  4. Pick what fits ONE week: your sprint
  5. Five-minute check-ins, twice a week
  6. Show each other working output weekly
  7. Ask "what do we improve?", then repeat
